# Engineering

*Written 2026-09-03.*

Much of the work here was planned from the outset to be public and inspectable. Most of it will probably never be useful to anyone as a product, and that's fine. Its purpose is different: to leave a concrete record of how seriously I take engineering and what it might be like to have me on a team.

It also gives me something professional work rarely does—a place to explore technologies without product pressure, organizational constraints, or somebody else's roadmap. That freedom does not mean I treat these projects as toys. Quite the opposite. I tend to make them more demanding than they strictly need to be, giving myself room to learn and grow under realistic conditions while still holding the implementation to the standards I would expect from production software.

I very much like the OSS community and enjoy thinking about what makes open-source development different. These projects, however, were never conceived as community projects. I never expected anyone else to work on them, nor built them with outside contributions in mind. Their being public is mostly about inspectability rather than collaboration.

That said, I can imagine that founding something which eventually grows into a living OSS community must be a great feeling. I'm happy to leave that possibility open.

Testing is part of the same mindset. Years of practicing TDD taught me not to treat a test suite as a finishing step, but as part of the software itself. Tests grow alongside the code and become a persistent support structure I can lean on when the system grows beyond what I can comfortably keep in my head. Knowing that they continue to guard the intended behavior gives me a great deal of peace of mind.

Nobody is standing behind me with a cane demanding any of this. The motivation is mostly internal. I've always been ambitious about my craft and wanted to understand what doing the job well actually requires—not just enough to make something work, but enough to trust what I've built.

Seeing how much I was able to build independently, while still holding myself to a high standard, also sharpened my appetite for further professional growth. It has even made me think more seriously about building commercial products of my own someday. So far, those remain thoughts rather than products.

The projects that follow are the public evidence of that work as an individual.
